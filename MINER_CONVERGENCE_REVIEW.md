## Passes 10–17: discrepancy, repair and comparison record

The primary turnaround was re-inspected throughout these passes. The pitch and hero images remain secondary references. Defining traits remain the broad battered hat, dark scruffy hair and beard, cream rolled-sleeve work shirt, open dark vest, red scarf, heavy worn trousers, tall boots and practical traveling equipment. The complete base costume is modeled and reviewed independently of the pack, satchel, canteen and tools.

The ranked ten discrepancies at the start of this continuation were: **(1)** rigid stance and arm silhouette; **(2)** regular trouser/boot cross-sections; **(3)** unweighted pack/roll silhouette; **(4)** inflated sleeves and uniform waist; **(5)** hip equipment scale, weight and construction; **(6)** generic head and face; **(7)** hand anatomy and grips; **(8)** graphic hair/beard masses; **(9)** regular hat construction; **(10)** weak material differentiation and structural wear. Those priorities were re-ranked after each render set rather than treated as a fixed detail checklist.

Each linked comparison contains the reference plus front, side, back and three-quarter renders of both base and expedition configurations. Each numbered checkpoint also has the same eight views as black silhouettes, saved as `pass_NN_LOADOUT_silhouette_VIEW.png`. Cameras, neutral lighting and background were held constant. The face close-ups are additional checks, not replacements for the four-view test.

### Pass 10 — stance, lower clothing and loaded silhouette

**Three repairs:** opened and lengthened the hanging arm stance, adjusted shoulder/head asymmetry and turned the feet outward; rebuilt the existing trousers and boot surfaces with changing cross-sections, knee/ankle compression and longer built toe forms; narrowed and seated the pack/roll closer to the back with sag and compression.

**Why / comparison:** the unequipped front and side made the rigid figure and tubular lower limbs especially apparent. Front and three-quarter became less mannequin-like, and the pack projected less from the back. The remaining hat size, boot/knee heights and equipment alignment became easier to judge.

**Next three:** hat/body scale; continuous trousers and boot-height relationship; pickaxe/pan/canteen alignment. [Four-view comparison](artifacts/miner-convergence/loadout_comparison_pass_10.jpg) · [Checkpoint](artifacts/miner-convergence/pass_10.blend)

### Pass 11 — measured proportions and tool arrangement

**Three repairs:** reduced the brim and crown width/height; raised the boot tops and knee landmarks and consolidated the existing trouser surfaces into a continuous garment with a recut open knee tear; corrected the pickaxe shaft/head orientation and refined pan and canteen scale/placement.

**Why / comparison:** these were larger perceptual changes than surface detail. Front and side now relate the head, legs and boots more closely to the sheet. The continuous trouser seat removes separate inflated-looking upper-leg joins. The pickaxe head is transverse near ground level and the pan reads as a hanging metal dish. ## Remaining repair plan — ranked from the latest renders

1. **Torso/shoulder silhouette and proportions:** remove the broad straight-sided garment read; resolve shoulder continuity, waist height and chest-to-waist taper against the primary sheet.
2. **Hat silhouette:** the brim/crown remain too regular; sculpt a convincing battered slouch with coherent front, side and rear shape.
3. **Pack/bedroll construction:** improve real attachment, asymmetric load, flatter constructed canvas faces and roll compression. Reassess bag depth in profile.
4. Gear grasp and placement: reconstruct believable hands, establish exact pick/rim contact, and refine satchel position while retaining four-view readability.
5. Face and hair: anatomically connect jaw, cheeks, nose and lids; replace the beard-mask and bob-like hair read with rugged scruff and irregular medium hair.
6. Clothing drape: replace remaining regular sectional folds with tension and compression driven by shoulders, belt, elbows and knees.
7. Boots: refine leather collapse, heel/toe construction and trouser overlap.
8. Main costume construction: correct vest armholes/lapels and scarf wrap so they resemble period garments rather than graphic panels.
9. Knee damage and structural wear: add only after the large-form gate passes.
10. Materials: proper linen/canvas/leather/wood/iron response and selective dust, sweat, mud and abrasion; no texture-noise camouflage.

**Intended next major pass: items 1–3 only**, then repeat the four neutral views and silhouettes and re-rank. Identity refinement follows according to its visual impact after those fixes.
